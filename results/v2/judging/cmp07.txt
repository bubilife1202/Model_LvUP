# Technical Design Memo: Global Event-Ingestion Pipeline

**To:** Engineering Leadership
**Subject:** Architecture for Global Event-Ingestion Pipeline
**Date:** 2026-06-12

---

## 1. Problem Statement and Assumptions

We require a globally distributed event-ingestion system sustaining 200,000 events/s across three active-active regions (us-east-1, eu-west-1, ap-southeast-1), with bursts to 300,000 events/s for up to 10 minutes. Events average 1 KiB; p95 is 4 KiB. Per-tenant ordering must be preserved; global ordering is not required. Exactly-once delivery semantics must hold across producer retries, worker crashes, and full regional loss.

**Assumptions:**
- Tenants are pre-sharded; tenant_id hashing is stable and known at ingest time.
- Producers are outside our control and may retry indefinitely with the same event ID.
- "Accepted" means durably written to at least two regions before acknowledging to the producer.
- Downstream consumers are stream processors reading from a durable log; consumer-side idempotency is separately enforced.
- A regional outage means all nodes in that region are unreachable for up to 72 hours.
- Clock skew across regions is bounded to 500ms (NTP + PTP).

---

## 2. Architecture

**Chosen: Partitioned Durable Log with Cross-Region Synchronous Replication and Centralized Deduplication Index**

Each region runs a Kafka cluster sized for local peak load. Inbound traffic is accepted by a stateless ingestion gateway that assigns a monotonic sequence number per tenant shard, checks a deduplication store, and writes to the local Kafka partition. Kafka is configured with `acks=all` and `min.insync.replicas=2`. Cross-region replication uses a synchronous two-phase commit via a thin coordination layer: an event is acknowledged to the producer only after it is committed in at least two of three regions.

A distributed deduplication store (Apache Cassandra with LWT—lightweight transactions) holds `(tenant_id, event_id) → (region, offset, timestamp)` with a 72-hour TTL matching our outage SLA. Each ingestion gateway performs a conditional insert: if the row already exists, the write is dropped and the original offset is returned to the producer.

**Rejected Alternative 1: Single-Region Primary with Async Fan-Out.** Simpler operationally but violates the exactly-once guarantee under primary failure: events acknowledged by the primary but not yet replicated are lost. Also introduces a single point of latency bottleneck.

**Rejected Alternative 2: CRDTs / Eventual-Consistency Merge.** Would allow independent writes in each region without coordination, but CRDTs cannot enforce exactly-once for non-commutative operations. Merging duplicate event IDs from split-brain partitions produces undefined ordering within a tenant's stream.

---

## 3. Capacity Plan

**Ingress bandwidth:**
- Sustained: 200,000 events/s × 1 KiB = 200 MiB/s globally; ~67 MiB/s per region.
- Burst (10 min): 300,000 × 1 KiB = 300 MiB/s; ~100 MiB/s per region.
- p95 sizing check: 200,000 × 0.05 × 4 KiB + 200,000 × 0.95 × 1 KiB = 40 MiB/s + 190 MiB/s = 230 MiB/s effective average bandwidth. Provision gateways for 350 MiB/s per region (1.5× p95 burst headroom).

**Kafka partitions per region:**
- Target: ≤5 MiB/s per partition (conservative Kafka guideline). 350 MiB/s ÷ 5 MiB/s = 70 partitions. Round to 128 for power-of-two alignment and future tenant growth.

**Durable storage (7-day retention):**
- 200 MiB/s × 86,400 s × 7 days = ~117 TiB raw per region. With 3× replication factor within a region: ~351 TiB per region. Three regions: ~1.05 PiB total. Using NVMe storage at $0.10/GiB/month ≈ $105K/month storage cost; use tiered storage (S3 offload after 24 hours) to reduce to ~$18K/month.

**Deduplication index:**
- 200,000 events/s × 72 h × 3,600 s/h = ~51.8 billion rows. Each row ≈ 64 bytes (IDs + offset + timestamp) = ~3.3 TiB per region. Cassandra with 3-node clusters per region handles this comfortably with bloom filters.

**Cross-region replication network:**
- Each event written twice cross-region: 200 MiB/s × 2 = 400 MiB/s inter-region egress per region. Three regions: 400 × 3 = 1.2 Gbps total inter-region. Provision 2 Gbps inter-region links per region pair with burst to 3 Gbps.

**Replay bandwidth:** Assuming 1% of events need replay: 2,000 events/s × 4 KiB (p95) = 8 MiB/s replay traffic, negligible against ingress headroom.

---

## 4. Backpressure Policy

**Low load (<50% capacity, <100 MiB/s per region):** Gateway accepts all events, targets p99 acknowledgment latency ≤50ms. No throttling.

**Medium load (50–80%, 100–160 MiB/s):** Gateway enables producer-side rate hints via response headers (`X-Ingest-Throttle: mild`). Producers reduce send rate by 10%. Deduplication store TTL check is moved off the hot path via async read-ahead cache.

**High load (80–95%, 160–190 MiB/s):** Gateway enforces per-tenant token buckets at 80% of each tenant's contracted rate. New connections beyond 10K concurrent are queued with a 2s timeout. Response latency SLO relaxed to p99 ≤200ms.

**Saturation (>95% or >190 MiB/s):** Gateway returns HTTP 429 to lowest-priority tenants (configurable tier). Core-tier tenants continue at reduced rate; surplus events are shed. No buffering beyond Kafka's own replication buffer to avoid compounding lag.

**During regional outage:** The two surviving regions each absorb ~50% of the failed region's traffic (load balancer failover via DNS/anycast within 30s). Capacity headroom was sized for this: 200 MiB/s total rerouted onto two regions at 100 MiB/s each, within burst capacity (100 MiB/s per region). The two-of-three quorum degrades to two-of-two (strict): no events acknowledged unless both surviving regions commit. This preserves exactly-once at the cost of higher latency. Tenants whose shard-leader was in the failed region enter a 5s election period; producers receive 503 and should retry with exponential backoff (max 60s).

---

## 5. Replay and Deduplication

Each event carries a producer-assigned `event_id` (UUIDv7, which encodes timestamp). The ingestion gateway performs:

1. **Dedup check:** Cassandra LWT `INSERT IF NOT EXISTS (tenant_id, event_id) VALUES (region, kafka_offset, now())`. On conflict, return 200 with original offset—idempotent acknowledgment.
2. **Write to Kafka:** If dedup succeeds, write to the tenant's assigned partition. Kafka offset is stored back into the Cassandra row atomically via a follow-up CAS update.
3. **Cross-region replication:** The coordination layer writes the event to both peer regions' Kafka clusters before sending final ACK to producer. Each peer region also upserts into its own dedup store.

**Checkpoints:** Downstream consumers commit Kafka offsets to a Zookeeper-backed offset store after processing. Consumers re-read from the last committed offset on restart; Cassandra dedup prevents double-processing effects downstream.

**Replay:** To replay a tenant's stream, consumers seek to a prior Kafka offset. The dedup store is not consulted during replay (replay is an intentional re-read); idempotency of downstream effects is the consumer's responsibility, enforced by documented consumer API contract.

---

## 6. Failover and Recovery

**Single-region loss:** DNS/anycast routes producers to surviving regions within 30s. The failed region's Kafka partitions have replicas in the other two regions (cross-region mirror topics with in-sync replica tracking). The surviving regions elect new leaders for orphaned partitions. No data loss because ACK required two-of-three commits. Recovery: when the region returns, it replays from the cross-region mirror to rebuild its local Kafka log, then rejoins quorum. Estimated rebuild time: 3.3 TiB dedup index + 24h Kafka retention = ~6 hours at 200 MiB/s ingest with 20% spare bandwidth for catch-up.

**Process crashes:** Kafka guarantees durability for committed offsets. The gateway is stateless; crashed instances are replaced by autoscaling within 60s. In-flight requests that had not received ACK will be retried by producers and caught by dedup.

**Producer retries:** Handled entirely by the dedup LWT. A retry with the same event_id receives a 200 with the original offset. Retries with different event_ids (producer bug) are accepted as new events.

---

## 7. Top Three Operational Risks

**Risk 1: Cassandra LWT contention at peak.** LWT throughput is limited to ~5K writes/s per partition. With 200K events/s and 128 partitions, that is ~1,562 LWT writes/s per partition—manageable, but a hot tenant could create a hot partition. **Mitigation:** Consistent hash sharding of the dedup keyspace by tenant_id with virtual nodes; circuit-break tenants exceeding 500 LWT/s/partition.

**Risk 2: Cross-region network partition causing ACK stall.** If the inter-region link degrades, two-of-three quorum stalls. **Mitigation:** Per-region circuit breaker; if quorum cannot be reached within 2s, gateway downgrades to one-of-three (local commit only) and marks events as "unconfirmed." These events are reconciled when connectivity restores via a background reconciliation job that replays unconfirmed offsets to peer regions and resolves dedup conflicts by event_id.

**Risk 3: Dedup TTL expiry before producer retry window closes.** A producer retrying an event after 72 hours will have its dedup record expired, causing re-acceptance. **Mitigation:** Producer SLA contract requires retries within 24 hours; producers exceeding this window receive a distinct error code (410 Gone) and must treat the event as permanently dropped or generate a new event_id.

MEMO: Technical Design — Global Event-Ingestion Pipeline

TO: Platform Engineering
FROM: Systems Architecture
DATE: 2026-06-12
SUBJECT: Architecture and Operational Design for High-Throughput Event Ingestion

---

**1. Problem Statement and Assumptions**

We must ingest events from multi-tenant producers at 200,000 events/second (EPS) sustained globally, peaking at 300,000 EPS for up to 10 minutes, across three active-active regions. Average event size is 1 KiB; P95 is 4 KiB. Per-tenant ordering must be preserved; global ordering is not required. Exactly-once semantics are required end-to-end.

Explicit assumptions: (a) Producers respect HTTP 429 responses. (b) Each region is an independent failure domain with local durable storage. (c) Cross-region replication latency is ≤150 ms under normal conditions. (d) Downstream consumers accept replay from any region. (e) Event IDs are globally unique UUIDs assigned by producers before transmission. (f) Tenant count is ≤100,000; per-tenant partition assignment is stable within a region.

---

**2. Chosen Architecture and Rejected Alternatives**

**Chosen: Partitioned log per region with cross-region async replication and distributed deduplication store.**

Each region runs a Kafka cluster partitioned by `tenant_id` hash (256 partitions per cluster), preserving per-tenant ordering without imposing global ordering. A stateless ingestion gateway performs deduplication using a Redis Cluster with atomic NX operations before writing to Kafka. Cross-region replication uses Kafka MirrorMaker 2, which queues durably when a remote region is unreachable and replays on reconnection. Downstream workers use Kafka transactional consumers with idempotent effect-store writes.

**Rejected Alternative 1 — Single global Kafka cluster.** A single cluster creates a replication bottleneck and blast radius spanning all tenants. Cross-continental latency (80–150 ms) forces large linger configurations hurting tail latency, and local writes become impossible during a network partition, violating the active-active requirement.

**Rejected Alternative 2 — Queue-per-tenant (SQS-style individual queues).** At 100,000 tenants this creates 100,000 queue resources per region; metadata overhead exceeds operational limits on most managed services. Partition compaction and offset management become unmanageable, and rebalancing during scale events is impractical.

---

**3. Capacity Plan**

**Blended average event size (P95 correction):**
0.95 × 1 KiB + 0.05 × 4 KiB = 0.95 + 0.20 = 1.15 KiB blended average.

**Per-region ingress (global load divided evenly across three regions):**
Sustained per region: (200,000 EPS / 3) × 1.15 KiB = 66,667 × 1.15 KiB = 76,667 KiB/s ≈ 74.9 MiB/s.
Peak per region: (300,000 EPS / 3) × 1.15 KiB = 100,000 × 1.15 KiB = 115,000 KiB/s ≈ 112.3 MiB/s.

**Durable storage rate (per region, 72-hour retention):**
Raw write rate: 74.9 MiB/s × 3,600 s/hr × 72 hr = 74.9 × 259,200 ≈ 19,414,080 MiB ≈ 18.5 TiB per replica.
With intra-region replication factor 3: 18.5 TiB × 3 = 55.5 TiB per region raw.
Provision 85 TiB per region → headroom: 85 / 55.5 − 1 ≈ 53%.

**Cross-region replication network:**
Each region replicates to two peers: 74.9 MiB/s × 2 = 149.8 MiB/s sustained egress per region.
Peak: 112.3 MiB/s × 2 = 224.6 MiB/s egress per region.
Provision 2 × 2 Gbps (each 2 Gbps ≈ 238.4 MiB/s; combined ≈ 476.8 MiB/s) dedicated inter-region links per region, yielding 2.1× headroom over peak egress.

**Headroom summary:** Storage +53%, network 2.1× over peak, gateway compute provisioned for 200,000 EPS per region (50% above single-region peak of 133,333 EPS) via horizontal auto-scaling.

---

**4. Backpressure Policy**

- **Low (<60% gateway capacity, <45 MiB/s per region):** Accept all events; HTTP 200, no throttling. Idempotency checks run synchronously inline.
- **Medium (60–80%, 45–60 MiB/s):** Return HTTP 429 with `Retry-After: 2` for the slowest 20% of tenants by current volume. Producer SDKs increase retry backoff by 2×. Internal buffer limits raised; no traffic shed yet.
- **High (80–95%, 60–71 MiB/s):** Enforce per-tenant rate limits based on partition backlog depth. Tenants exceeding fair-share quota receive HTTP 429 with `Retry-After: 5`. Premium tenants receive 2× allocation before shedding begins. Alert on-call.
- **Saturation (>95%, >71 MiB/s or producer ack timeout):** Shed lowest-priority tenants with HTTP 503. Pin critical tenants to reserved-capacity partitions. Dead-letter queue captures rejected event metadata for producer reconciliation.

**During regional outage:** Each surviving region absorbs half of the lost region's load: 74.9 MiB/s + (74.9 MiB/s / 2) = 74.9 + 37.5 = 112.4 MiB/s, equal to provisioned peak capacity. Per-region EPS rises from 66,667 to 100,000, which is 50% of the 200,000 EPS gateway provision. Medium-load policy activates immediately on surviving regions regardless of local metrics, preventing lag accumulation before auto-scale completes. Anycast DNS reroutes producers within 30 seconds. Replication to the offline region queues durably on MirrorMaker 2 and replays automatically on reconnection.

---

**5. Replay and Deduplication Strategy**

Each event carries a producer-assigned `event_id` (UUID v4). At the ingestion gateway, before writing to Kafka, an atomic Redis `SET event_id 1 NX PX 259200000` (72-hour TTL matching retention) executes. If the key exists, the gateway returns HTTP 200 without producing to Kafka, blocking duplicate ingestion.

If absent, the event is written to Kafka inside a producer transaction. Downstream workers use Kafka transactional consumers: the consumer reads a batch, writes effects via `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (event_id) DO NOTHING`, then commits the Kafka offset atomically in the same transaction. If a worker crashes before committing, it re-reads from the last committed offset; the idempotent insert absorbs re-processing without producing duplicate downstream effects. Consumer group offsets are checkpointed every 5 seconds, bounding replay to at most 5 seconds of events on restart.

Cross-region deduplication: before writing to a peer region's local Kafka, the regional gateway executes the same Redis NX check on the destination store, preventing double-writes from concurrent multi-region producers.

---

**6. Failover and Recovery**

**Single-region loss:** Anycast DNS shifts producers to surviving regions within 30 seconds (health-check interval 10 s, TTL 20 s). MirrorMaker 2 maintains continuous replicas of all topic partitions; consumers on surviving regions continue from replicated offsets with no gap for already-mirrored events. On recovery, MirrorMaker 2 re-syncs the delta; the recovering region replays through the effect store, which absorbs all duplicates via `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, before rejoining the active-active ring.

**Process crashes:** Kafka leader election promotes a replica within 10 seconds. Gateway instances are stateless; the load balancer removes unhealthy instances within 5 seconds. Workers restart from last committed checkpoint, re-processing at most 5 seconds of events; transactional writes ensure no partial effects are visible.

**Producer retries:** Producers include the original `event_id` on every retry. The Redis NX gate returns HTTP 200 for recognized duplicates; no duplicate Kafka message is produced and no duplicate downstream effect occurs. Producers using `enable.idempotence=true` also receive broker-side deduplication via producer epoch and sequence number.

---

**7. Top Three Operational Risks and Mitigations**

**Risk 1 — Idempotency store failure opens duplicate path.** If Redis becomes unavailable, duplicate filtering fails. Mitigation: Deploy Redis Cluster with 3 primaries and synchronous replicas per AZ. Configure the gateway to fail closed (HTTP 503) rather than fail open when the store is unreachable, accepting brief unavailability over a correctness violation. Alert on Redis p99 latency exceeding 10 ms; run quarterly game-day exercises killing nodes under peak load.

**Risk 2 — Replication lag exceeding TTL during extended partition.** If cross-region replication pauses beyond 72 hours, idempotency store TTLs expire, enabling duplicate writes on replay. Mitigation: Alert at 48-hour lag; automatically extend TTL to 168 hours when lag exceeds 12 hours; enforce a mandatory deduplication reconciliation job before marking a recovered region writable.

**Risk 3 — Hot-tenant partition skew saturating individual brokers.** A tenant generating disproportionate volume concentrates traffic on one partition. Mitigation: Detect tenants exceeding 2,000 EPS per region and apply sub-tenant sharding via a rotating shard suffix (tenant_id:0, tenant_id:1, ...), preserving ordering within each shard. Monitor per-partition byte rate; auto-rebalance when any partition exceeds 80% utilization for 5 consecutive minutes.
